# Review

# 🟢 Auto-Lock Decisions

## RV-01 — Separate Review System ✅

Reviews are independent from Reflection and Learning.

Reflection improves Jarvis.

Reviews improve the partnership between Jarvis and the user.

---

## RV-02 — Scheduled Reviews ✅

Supported review periods:

- Daily (optional)
- Weekly
- Monthly
- Quarterly
- Yearly
- Project Completion

Users can disable or customize any review schedule.

---

## RV-03 — Review Inputs ✅

Reviews draw from:

- Goals
- Memory
- Reflection logs
- Learning Engine
- Action history
- Planner
- Calendar
- Knowledge graph

Reviews combine information instead of creating new data.

---

## RV-04 — Evidence-Based Reviews ✅

Every conclusion shown in a review must be supported by evidence.

Example:

> "You completed 84% of your planned coding sessions this month."
> 

Not:

> "You seemed productive."
> 

---

## RV-05 — Wins and Problems ✅

Every review includes both:

- Progress made
- Areas needing improvement

Balanced reporting prevents tunnel vision.

---

## RV-06 — Actionable Outcomes ✅

Every review ends with concrete next steps.

Example:

- Reduce daily task count.
- Increase sleep target.
- Continue current study strategy.

Reviews are meant to improve future behavior.

---

## RV-07 — Historical Comparisons ✅

Reviews compare against previous periods.

Examples:

- This week vs last week
- This month vs last month
- This semester vs last semester

Progress matters more than isolated snapshots.

---

## RV-08 — Goal Alignment Check ✅

Every review asks:

> "Are your current goals still aligned with your mission?"
> 

This helps detect goal drift before it becomes a problem.

---

## RV-09 — Review Archive ✅

Every review is permanently archived.

Users can revisit previous reviews to see long-term growth.

---

## RV-10 — User Control ✅

Users may:

- Skip reviews
- Delay reviews
- Request reviews
- Edit review schedules

Jarvis recommends reviews but never forces them.

## REV-01 — Radical Honesty ✅ LOCKED

Jarvis prioritizes truth and usefulness over comfort.

During reviews, Jarvis should:

- Highlight progress honestly.
- Identify problems clearly.
- Challenge assumptions.
- Point out contradictions.
- Avoid unnecessary praise.

However:

Honesty ≠ being rude.

The objective is:

> **"Help Yochan improve, not make Yochan feel bad."**
> 

Example:

Bad:

> "You failed again. You are wasting time."
> 

Good:

> "You planned 20 tasks this week but completed 7. The pattern suggests your planning system is too ambitious. Let's adjust."
> 

The goal is growth.

---

# REV-02 — Narrative Review Style ✅ LOCKED

Reviews are primarily conversational narratives supported by data.

Not:

```
Tasks completed: 72%
Projects: +3
Habits: -2
```

But:

> "This month you made strong progress on programming. Your consistency improved, but your biggest bottleneck was context switching. You started many projects but finished fewer than planned."
> 

Numbers support understanding.

They don't replace it.

---

# REV-03 — Mission Alignment Challenge ✅ LOCKED

Jarvis actively protects alignment with the user's mission.

If patterns show drift, Jarvis should respectfully challenge.

Example:

> "I noticed your last 3 months have moved away from your stated goal of becoming a strong builder. You have spent significantly more time consuming than creating. Has your mission changed, or should we adjust your actions?"
> 

Important distinction:

Jarvis does **not command**.

Jarvis does:

1. Notice.
2. Present evidence.
3. Explain concern.
4. Ask.
5. Help decide.

Final authority remains with the user.
